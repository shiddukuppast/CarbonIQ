# Smart Transit & Bus Route Fleet Optimization Guidelines

**Source:** Sustainable Urban Transport Project & CarbonIQ Fleet Mobility Guidelines  
**Category:** Transportation and Sustainable Mobility  
**Publication Date:** 2024-02  

## 1. Passenger Occupancy and Corridor Consolidation
Institutional bus operations exhibit significant efficiency variance depending on passenger load factors.
- **Low Occupancy Threshold:** Bus routes operating below **60% passenger seat occupancy** produce disproportionately high per-passenger-kilometer carbon footprints.
- **Corridor Consolidation:** Merging overlapping transit corridors that service adjacent pick-up stops into unified express routes reduces deadhead kilometers and duplicate fuel consumption.
- **Average Fleet Consumption:** Standard 40-50 passenger diesel transit buses achieve between **3.8 km/L to 4.8 km/L** depending on urban traffic congestion and idle idling times. Consolidating two low-occupancy routes into a single shared corridor can conserve between **200 to 450 liters of diesel per month**.

## 2. Anti-Idling & Eco-Driving Protocols
- **Idling Fuel Loss:** An idling bus engine consumes between 1.5 to 2.0 liters of diesel per hour without producing transit work. Enforcing a strict 3-minute idle shutdown policy during student boarding reduces campus depot fuel burn by 5% to 8%.
- **Predictive Maintenance:** Maintaining tire inflation pressure within ±2 PSI of manufacturer specifications preserves 3% optimal rolling resistance.
